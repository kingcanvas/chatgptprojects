# Shardstead Development Plan

## 1. Project definition

**Working title:** Shardstead  
**Genre:** Endless 3D sandbox, building, exploration, and progression  
**Platforms:** PC and mobile web browsers, installable as a Progressive Web App where supported  
**Current phase:** Concept and planning

Shardstead should give players the freedom to set their own goals. There is no required final level or ending. The game continues by generating new areas, resources, discoveries, and building opportunities as the player explores.

## 2. Game identity

The world is divided into enormous regions and unusual land shards connected by glowing rifts. Every player begins near a dormant **World Core**. The Core serves as a home anchor, progression system, and gateway to new types of terrain.

Upgrading the World Core can unlock:

- New biomes and terrain features
- New ores, plants, and crafting materials
- More advanced tools and building pieces
- Portals to distant land shards
- New creatures, weather, and world events
- Automation and transportation systems

This World Core system is the main feature that separates Shardstead from other block-building sandbox games.

## 3. Player activities

Players should be able to mix activities instead of selecting a permanent class.

- Explore the surface, caves, ruins, and distant shards
- Gather wood, stone, ore, plants, and rare materials
- Place and remove world pieces
- Construct shelters, homes, farms, towns, machines, roads, and monuments
- Craft and upgrade tools, storage, equipment, and transportation
- Farm renewable resources
- Discover creatures and environmental hazards
- Improve the World Core and unlock new possibilities

## 4. World generation

The world should feel endless while remaining efficient enough for phones.

Planned approach:

- Generate terrain in chunks around the player
- Save player-modified chunks and regenerate untouched terrain from a world seed
- Stream nearby chunks in and unload distant chunks
- Use multiple biomes with distinct terrain, materials, plants, and atmosphere
- Generate caves and points of interest separately from surface terrain
- Introduce rare land shards through rifts instead of placing every feature in the starting region
- Use level-of-detail and simplified distant geometry when necessary

The first prototype will use a deliberately small set of blocks and one biome so movement, saving, and performance can be proven before content expands.

## 5. Controls and cameras

### PC

- WASD movement
- Mouse camera control
- Space to jump
- Primary action for breaking or using
- Secondary action for placing or alternate use
- Number keys or mouse wheel for the hotbar
- Inventory and pause/settings shortcuts
- F5 to cycle first-person, third-person-back, and third-person-front views

### Mobile

- Virtual movement control
- Swipe-to-look camera control
- On-screen jump and context-sensitive action buttons
- Touch hotbar and inventory
- On-screen camera button matching the PC F5 behavior
- Adjustable control size, opacity, and placement

All important PC actions must have a touch equivalent. Interface elements must respect small screens, safe areas, landscape orientation, and different aspect ratios.

## 6. Rendering and graphics settings

The game will offer simple presets plus individual controls.

| Setting | Auto | Low | Medium | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resolution scale | Device-based | Reduced | Balanced | Native or near-native |
| Render distance | Device-based | Short | Medium | Long |
| Shadows | Device-based | Off or minimal | Limited | Detailed |
| Particles | Device-based | Reduced | Standard | Full |
| Lighting effects | Device-based | Basic | Balanced | Enhanced |
| Geometry detail | Device-based | Simplified | Standard | Detailed |

Additional player settings:

- Frame-rate limit
- Field of view
- Camera sensitivity
- View bobbing
- Motion and screen-shake intensity
- Shadow quality
- Particle amount
- Render distance
- Resolution scale

Auto mode should detect performance and lower or raise expensive effects gradually. The initial performance goals are a stable 30 FPS on supported mobile devices and 60 FPS on typical supported PCs, with graceful reductions when a device cannot maintain its target.

## 7. Visual content policy

All project-specific 3D models, textures, icons, interface images, and promotional visuals will be original creations made for Shardstead.

Content methods may include:

- Procedural geometry generated by game code
- Original low-poly or voxel-style models exported for the game
- Original generated textures and material maps
- Original interface graphics and icons
- Reusable modular pieces created specifically for Shardstead

No models, textures, or images will be copied from Minecraft or another game. External technical libraries may be used under compatible licenses, but they will not supply Shardstead's creative identity.

## 8. Saving

The first version should support a reliable local single-player save containing:

- World seed
- Player position and settings
- Inventory and progression
- World Core level
- Modified chunks and placed structures

Cloud saves and accounts can be considered later. Save formats should include a version number so future updates can migrate older worlds.

## 9. Development phases

### Phase 0 — Planning

- Approve the game concept and name
- Define the first visual direction
- Choose the browser 3D technology after a small performance test
- Decide the first biome and starter materials

### Phase 1 — Playable foundation

- Desktop and mobile movement
- First- and third-person cameras
- One generated terrain biome
- Chunk loading and unloading
- Place and remove basic world pieces
- Basic hotbar and inventory
- Local save and load
- Graphics presets and essential settings

### Phase 2 — Building and progression

- Crafting
- Tool upgrades
- World Core levels
- More building materials
- Basic farming and renewable resources
- Additional biomes

### Phase 3 — Exploration systems

- Caves and ruins
- Rifts and rare land shards
- Creatures and environmental hazards
- Weather and day/night cycle
- Transportation and automation prototypes

### Phase 4 — Polish and expansion

- Expanded accessibility and control customization
- Performance profiling across phones and PCs
- Improved lighting, effects, sound, and animation
- More content and long-term progression
- Installable PWA packaging and release preparation

### Later consideration — Multiplayer

Multiplayer should not be added until the single-player world, saving, controls, and performance are stable. Planning for clean game-state boundaries early will make a future multiplayer experiment easier without forcing it into the first release.

## 10. First playable milestone

The first milestone is successful when a player can:

1. Open the game on a PC or phone.
2. Enter a generated 3D world.
3. Move, jump, and switch camera views.
4. Break and place several basic world pieces.
5. Change graphics settings.
6. Leave and return without losing their saved changes.

This milestone should be small, stable, and measurable. More content should be added only after it works well on both platform types.

## 11. Decisions still requiring approval

- Final visual palette and atmosphere
- Peaceful-only gameplay versus optional survival and combat
- First biome theme
- Character appearance and customization
- How quickly World Core upgrades unlock new content
- Whether early builds should be private or publicly playable
